# Three-season baseline: player salary comparisons

This table uses the 2025–26 holdout. A positive gap means the recorded salary was above the model estimate; a negative gap means it was below. The baseline model uses prior-season box-score stats and availability, so these gaps are leads to review—not judgments about a contract.

| Player | Recorded salary | Model estimate | Gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Paul George | $51.7M | $20.3M | +$31.3M |
| Stephen Curry | $59.6M | $30.5M | +$29.1M |
| Jaylen Brown | $53.1M | $27.2M | +$26.0M |
| Lauri Markkanen | $46.4M | $21.9M | +$24.5M |
| Joel Embiid | $55.2M | $31.8M | +$23.4M |

| Cam Thomas | $6.9M | $28.7M | −$21.8M |
| Jalen Williams | $6.6M | $27.6M | −$21.1M |
| Paolo Banchero | $15.3M | $33.4M | −$18.0M |
| Keyonte George | $4.3M | $20.0M | −$15.7M |

These differences show where recent stats and availability tell a different story from recorded salary. Contract rules, service time, extensions, awards, injuries, team needs, and salary-cap conditions may explain the gap; this model does not account for them. Check those details before drawing a conclusion.

The salary data can have more than one team entry for a traded player. The pipeline adds the team amounts together before making the comparison.
